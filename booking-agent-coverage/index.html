Security · identity

The guest chat trusts whatever email a visitor types

Booking as a guest resolves the customer by email alone, with no proof of who's actually typing — by design, so a visitor never has to sign up just to get a quote. That means anyone who knows a real customer's email can chat anonymously, book a job at an address of their own choosing, and have it attributed to that customer's real account — inheriting their saved payment preference and denormalized ACH details onto a job they never asked for.

This is the same class of issue already tracked for the public booking form (security_public_booking_email_impersonation.md), where an earlier fix attempt was reverted for breaking legitimate signed-in customers on a route that served both anonymous and authenticated traffic. The chat path is different — a guest embed session is always anonymous, so that specific failure mode may not apply — but the tradeoff (how to verify a returning guest without adding signup friction) is a product decision, not a mechanical bug fix.

Known gaps — not yet built

lower priority

Nothing here is broken so much as missing. Listed for visibility, not urgency.

Resume shows only 30 messages
GET /conversations/:id truncates to the last 30 for display. The model still has the full history — this only reads as amnesia in the UI.
Hard 30-turn cap
Stops a conversation at 30 turns regardless of how many tokens were actually used — an abuse ceiling that could stand to be a memory-aware limit instead.
No modify or cancel via chat
Once a job is confirmed, changing or cancelling it has to happen outside the conversation.
No provider preference
A customer can't ask for (or avoid) a specific provider through the chat.
No discount codes
The agent has no tool for applying a promo code to a quote.
No general human handoff
Only a narrow, rate-limited zip-check path exists — nothing for "let me talk to a person" more broadly.
No returning-customer memory
No read_customer_history tool yet — the agent can't say "same as your March booking?" from past jobs on file.
